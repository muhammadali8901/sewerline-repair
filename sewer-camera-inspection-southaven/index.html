What is a Sewer Camera Inspection and Why Is It Necessary?

A sewer camera inspection is a non-destructive diagnostic procedure where an optical fiber-optic camera head is guided through a property’s underground lateral pipe to inspect its internal condition in real time. Because sewer pipes lie four to eight feet beneath Southaven lawns and driveways, visual inspection eliminates speculative digging. It accurately pinpoints whether recurring backups stem from simple grease buildup, aggressive tree root penetration, shifting joints from DeSoto County clay soils, or broken pipe walls. Performing a video check is essential before deciding whether your home requires localized jetting, trenchless sewer repair, or comprehensive sewer line replacement.

How Video Sewer Inspections Work

When diagnosing a suspected blockage, the technician locates your home's exterior cleanout—frequently positioned near the foundation or along the front landscaping. A motorized camera reel fitted with high-output LED emitters is introduced directly into the line.

As the lens navigates through the private sewer lateral toward the Southaven municipal main, an integrated electronic radio transmitter (sonde) emits a signal. Using a handheld digital receiver on the surface, the specialist traces the camera’s exact path above ground, marking both the horizontal position and the precise depth of any defect.

This level of diagnostic clarity is vital when coordinating sewer line repair in Southaven, as it guarantees any planned excavation or trenchless installation targets only the affected footage, sparing pristine landscaping and concrete driveways from unnecessary demolition.

What Sewer Cameras Uncover Inside Southaven Pipes

  • Invasive Tree Root Networks: Fine hair-like roots enter microscopic bell-and-spigot joints in older clay or cast iron pipes, blooming into thick masses that trap toilet paper and solids.
  • Pipe Bellies & Sagging: Expansive clay soils in DeSoto County frequently cause localized ground settlement, creating dipped pipe valleys where standing wastewater accumulates sediment.
  • Longitudinal Cracks & Shear Breaks: Foundation shifting and soil expansion fracture rigid piping materials, allowing wastewater to seep into the surrounding subsoil.
  • Severe Scale & Tuberculation: Older cast iron laterals in neighborhoods like Greenbrook often suffer from calcification and rusted scale that snag debris.
  • Foreign Object Obstructions: Pinpoints flushed non-dispersible wipes, feminine products, or construction debris trapped in bends.

When Should You Request a Camera Inspection?
