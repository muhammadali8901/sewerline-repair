No-Dig vs. Open Trench

Trenchless vs. Traditional Sewer Repair

Two ways to fix the same broken pipe — one barely disturbs your yard, the other opens a trench. Here is how they compare on cost, time, and durability, and when digging is genuinely the right call in Southaven.

Discuss My Options: (662) 653-8071

Which Is Better: Trenchless or Traditional?

For most cracked-but-intact pipes, trenchless wins on total cost and disruption — no demolished driveway or lawn to rebuild. Traditional excavation wins when the pipe has fully collapsed, lost grade, or needs resizing. The honest answer for your home comes from a camera inspection, not a sales pitch.

How Trenchless Sewer Repair Works

Trenchless methods fix the pipe from the inside. In cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining, an epoxy-saturated liner is inverted into the old pipe through an existing cleanout and cured into a seamless structural pipe. In pipe bursting, a bursting head fractures the old line outward while pulling a brand-new HDPE pipe into its place. Either way, the work happens through one or two small access points — no continuous trench.

How Traditional Excavation Works

Traditional replacement opens a trench along the full length of the lateral, removes the failed pipe, and installs new Schedule 40 PVC from house to city connection — then backfills, compacts, and restores the surface. It is the oldest method and still the right one when the pipe's structure is gone.

Head-to-Head: Cost, Time, and Disruption

Per-foot equipment pricing is surprisingly close: trenchless lining runs $80–$250 per foot, and open excavation sits in a similar band for the pipe work itself. The difference is everything around the pipe. Excavation adds demolition and rebuilding of driveways, sidewalks, landscaping, patios, and irrigation — often thousands of dollars that trenchless never incurs. That is why trenchless usually wins on total project cost. See our 2026 Southaven cost guide for full price ranges.

On time, trenchless lining or bursting typically finishes in one to two days; a full excavation with surface restoration can stretch toward a week. On durability, both deliver decades of service — quality CIPP liners carry a 50-year design life, and new PVC laterals last 50 to 100 years.

When Digging Is Actually the Right Choice

Trenchless is not universal. Open excavation is the honest recommendation when the pipe has fully collapsed (a liner needs a host pipe to form against), when there is severe grade loss — deep bellies holding standing water that lining cannot correct — or when the lateral needs resizing or rerouting. A reputable contractor will show you the camera footage and explain why before recommending the trench.

The Southaven Factor

Two local realities tilt many Southaven jobs toward trenchless. First, expansive clay soil: it makes deep trenching slower and messier, and backfilled trenches in clay are prone to settlement. Second, what sits above the pipe — mature oaks, irrigation, brick mailboxes, and long concrete driveways are expensive to demolish and rebuild. When the pipe qualifies, avoiding the trench avoids the two biggest cost multipliers in DeSoto County.

Trenchless vs. Traditional at a Glance

  • Total cost: trenchless usually lower — no surface restoration bills.
  • Project time: 1–2 days trenchless vs. up to a week for dig + restore.
  • Yard & driveway: trenchless preserves them; excavation removes and rebuilds.
  • Lifespan: 50-year design life (CIPP) vs. 50–100 years (new PVC).
  • Best for: trenchless suits cracked-but-intact pipes; digging suits collapsed or misgraded lines.
