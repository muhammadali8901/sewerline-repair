Sewer Line Repair Cost in Southaven, MS

Wondering what a sewer line repair will set you back? Below are the realistic price ranges Southaven homeowners pay in 2026 — from a $150 camera inspection to full lateral replacement — plus the factors that push your bill up or down, and how to avoid overpaying.

The Short Answer: What Will I Pay?

Most Southaven sewer jobs fall into four bands: a camera inspection ($150–$400) to diagnose the problem, hydro jetting ($350–$800) to clear it, trenchless lining ($80–$250 per foot, typically $3,000–$9,000 total) to rehabilitate the pipe without digging, or full replacement ($4,000–$12,000+) when the line is collapsed or fully deteriorated. Your exact price depends on depth, length, pipe material, and how much surface — driveway, landscaping, patio — sits above the line.

Sewer Line Repair Prices in Southaven: Service by Service

Video Camera Inspection: $150–$400

Every honest repair starts here. A technician feeds a high-definition camera through your cleanout to locate cracks, bellies, offsets, and root masses — and to show you the footage. This one step prevents the most expensive mistake in sewer work: paying for a full replacement when a targeted pipe repair would have done the job. Price varies with cleanout accessibility; homes without an existing cleanout may need one installed first.

Hydro Jetting and Sewer Cleaning: $350–$800

When the pipe is structurally sound but choked with grease, scale, or sludge, hydro jetting scours the full interior diameter with up to 4,000 PSI of water. It is the right fix for recurring slow drains and the wrong fix for a collapsed pipe — which is exactly why the camera inspection comes first.

Trenchless CIPP Lining: $80–$250 per Foot ($3,000–$9,000 Total)

Trenchless cured-in-place lining installs a seamless epoxy pipe inside your existing line through a single access point. Per-foot pricing looks similar to digging, but the total project usually costs thousands less because there is no driveway, sod, patio, or irrigation to demolish and rebuild afterward. Most residential liners carry a 50-year design life.

Full Sewer Line Replacement: $4,000–$12,000+

When the lateral has collapsed, lost grade, or deteriorated along most of its length, complete replacement excavates the old line and installs code-compliant Schedule 40 PVC from house to city connection, with permits and inspections through the City of Southaven. Depth is the biggest cost driver: a 4-foot-deep lateral is a fundamentally cheaper dig than one at 9 feet under a concrete driveway.

What Pushes Your Sewer Bill Up (or Down)

  • Depth and length: deeper, longer laterals mean more excavation hours and more pipe.
  • What's above the pipe: open lawn is cheap to restore; driveways, retaining walls, and brick patios are not.
  • Pipe material and soil: Southaven's expansive clay soils shift older cast iron and clay pipes, often turning a simple crack into an offset that needs more work.
  • Permits and tie-in location: replacements need city plan review and inspection; cutting into the municipal roadway costs more than a clean easement tie-in.
  • Timing: a $500 preventive cleaning beats a $9,000 emergency replacement every time. Slow drains ignored for a year become collapsed pipes.

How to Avoid Overpaying for Sewer Work

Insist on seeing the camera footage. Any contractor quoting thousands without showing you exactly what is wrong inside the pipe is asking you to buy blind. Ask about trenchless first. If the pipe is cracked but still round, lining or bursting usually beats open excavation on total cost. Get the repair matched to the damage — a single bad joint deserves a spot repair, not a whole new lateral. And check your insurance: many Mississippi homeowners carry a service-line endorsement that covers lateral repairs for a few dollars a month, but almost nobody asks about it until after the backup.
