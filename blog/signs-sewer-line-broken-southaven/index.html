Know the Warning Signs

7 Signs Your Sewer Line Is Broken

A broken sewer lateral rarely fails without warning. Here are the seven signs Southaven homeowners notice first — and what each one is telling you about the pipe buried under your yard.

Call About Symptoms: (662) 653-8071

Broken Line or Just a Clog?

A single slow drain is usually a local clog you can plunge. A broken or failing main sewer line affects multiple fixtures at once — slow drains throughout the house, gurgling toilets, sewage odors, or soggy patches in the yard. When several of these appear together, the problem is in the lateral, and a camera inspection is the fastest way to confirm it.

The 7 Warning Signs of a Broken Sewer Line

1. Multiple Drains Slowing Down at Once

One slow sink is a local clog. When the kitchen sink, the shower, and the toilet all drain slowly at the same time, the problem is downstream in the main lateral they share. This is the single most reliable sign the issue is in your sewer line, not a fixture.

2. Sewage Odors Inside or Outside Your Home

A healthy sewer line is airtight. Persistent rotten-egg or sewage smells from drains — or a foul patch in the yard — mean a cracked pipe or separated joint is venting sewer gas where it should not.

3. Gurgling Sounds From Toilets and Drains

Gurgling when you flush or run water means air is trapped by a partial blockage, belly, or break downstream. It is the sound of your drainage system struggling to breathe through a damaged section.

4. Soggy or Sunken Patches in the Yard

A break in the lateral leaks wastewater into the surrounding soil, creating perpetually soggy spots or visible depressions along the pipe's path — even weeks after the last rain. In Southaven's clay soil these often appear between the house and the street.

5. Unusually Lush, Green Patches of Grass

Sewage is fertilizer. A strip of grass that is suddenly greener and faster-growing than the rest of the lawn is often growing directly above a leaking lateral.

6. Sewage Backing Up Into Tubs and Showers

When the main line is blocked or collapsed, wastewater takes the lowest available exit — usually a ground-floor tub or shower. Any backup of this kind is an emergency: stop using water and call immediately.

7. Cracks in the Foundation or Driveway Settlement

A long-running leak erodes the soil supporting slabs and foundations. New cracks, or a driveway section that has settled near the sewer path, can trace back to a lateral that has been leaking for months.

What To Do When You Spot These Signs

Do not pour chemical drain cleaners down the line. Caustic chemicals rarely reach a main-line break, can damage older pipes further, and create a hazard for the technician who eventually opens the line. Do not ignore it and hope it clears — main-line damage only worsens, and a $400 inspection today beats a collapsed-line replacement later.

The right next step is a video camera inspection: it shows exactly what is wrong and where, so any pipe repair or replacement quote is based on evidence, not guesswork.

Break or Just a Clog? Quick Check

  • One slow fixture — probably a local clog. Try a plunger or call a plumber.
  • Several fixtures affected — likely the main lateral. Get a camera inspection.
  • Bad smells + slow drains — cracked pipe or broken seal. Do not wait.
  • Any sewage backup — emergency. Stop running water and call now.
