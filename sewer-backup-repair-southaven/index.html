What Causes a Sewer Backup and What Should You Do Immediately?

A sewer backup occurs when the primary underground lateral pipe carrying wastewater from your home to the municipal sewer is blocked or broken, causing contaminated water to reverse flow into the lowest drains (bathtubs, showers, and basement/slab fixtures). If this occurs, immediately stop running all water in the house, keep family members and pets away from contaminated areas, and avoid pouring commercial chemical cleaners down sluggish drains. Once safe, contact an underground plumbing technician who can access the exterior cleanout, relieve hydraulic line pressure, perform a video inspection, and complete necessary sewer line repair in Southaven.

The Critical Difference Between a Clogged Fixture and a Main Line Backup

If a single sink or toilet is running slowly, the restriction is typically confined to that specific fixture's trap arm. However, when using the washing machine causes dirty water to overflow your bathroom shower, or flushing an upstairs toilet makes the ground-floor toilet bubble violently, the problem is situated squarely within your main 4-inch underground sewer lateral.

In Southaven neighborhoods such as Central Park and Plum Point, heavy seasonal rainfall combined with dense DeSoto County clay soil can create localized ground shifting that shears underground pipes or allows stormwater infiltration to overwhelm compromised joints. Addressing this requires professional cable augers, hydro jetting, or structural pipe repair.

Immediate Steps to Protect Your Home During a Backup

  1. Cease All Water Flow: Every cup of water flushed, washed down a sink, or discharged from an appliance adds directly to the overflow pooling inside your home.
  2. Do Not Use Chemical Cleaners: Caustic drain products fail against mainline blockages and create hazardous chemical vapor pockets that pose risks to you and technicians.
  3. Check the Exterior Cleanout: If you have an outdoor ground-level sewer cleanout cap, carefully unscrewing it can occasionally divert backing wastewater onto the yard instead of your interior floors.
  4. Connect with a Sewer Specialist: Arrange an emergency mainline diagnosis to prevent permanent subfloor and drywall blackwater damage.

Long-Term Protection: Backwater Valves

Tired of worrying about sewage backing up during heavy Mississippi thunderstorms? Ask a technician about installing a backwater prevention valve.

  • One-Way Mechanical Flap: Allows household wastewater to exit freely into the city main.
  • Automatic Storm Surge Protection: Slams shut if municipal street mains become overwhelmed with rainwater.
  • DeSoto County Code Approved: Installed with an exterior access sleeve for simple annual maintenance.
